The question

A robot with a bank balance

Almost every robot in the world is a cost centre. Somebody buys it, somebody powers it, and the value it produces shows up on a different line of a different spreadsheet. The ROBOPRENEUR line of work at the IE Robotics and AI Lab asks what happens if you close that loop: give the robot an account, let it sell its work, and make it pay for its own upkeep out of what it earns.

Two of their experiments matter here. RODEO put robot work on a blockchain, with payment held in escrow until evidence of completion is verified. iTrash put a camera and a light ring on a set of recycling bins and paid people for sorting correctly.

I work on that line as a research assistant at the lab, where I built the agent based simulation of the ROBOPRENEUR economy. This project is a separate thing: replicating both experiments at a collection yard in the eastern Colombian Andes, with a different waste code, a different electricity supply, intermittent uplink, and nobody on site whose job is babysitting a research prototype.

Everything on this page runs. The simulation below is the model itself, not a recording of one, and the verification service really does verify.

That line is both the study's data and the evidence a robot attaches to its proof of work. The expected field is what the station lit; the bin field is what the person actually did.

Colombia's colour code, not the lab's

The original used a European blue, yellow and brown scheme. Since 2021, Colombia has a single national code under Resolución 2184 de 2019: blanco for aprovechables, verde for orgánicos, negro for no aprovechables. A station that teaches the wrong code is worse than no station.

Some sessions pay, some do not

The arm is decided by a coin flip before the item is shown, and it is written into the row with the outcome. The published iTrash study found a large accuracy gain but could not attribute it to the reward, because almost nobody claimed one. Randomising up front is what makes that question answerable at all.

Unsure is an answer

Below a confidence threshold, and for any material outside the taxonomy, the station lights nothing and says so. A station that confidently sends a battery to the organics bin is worse than one that admits it does not know.

Why the digest is on chain

When the robot submits a proof it commits the file's sha256 in the same transaction. The oracle replays the file the robot committed to, or it refuses. Without that, evidence could be edited after the fact and a replay would still pass, which is exactly the spoofing weakness the original paper flags as open.

Every rejection carries its numbers

Not "REJECTED" but "base travelled: 1.01 m recorded, 5.00 m required". The same line goes on chain as the rejection reason, so a member whose money did not move can read why, months later, from the ledger alone.

The number worth knowing first

How much work does a site need?

Seven seeds at each demand level, three simulated days each, run here and now. Everything below the line is a site where this should not be attempted.

Ready.

The shape is the point. Solvency is not gradual: below about half a job an hour the robot spends most of the run flat, which also means the jobs that do arrive go unanswered, which makes it poorer still. Above roughly 0.8 jobs an hour every seed pays for itself. Past two jobs an hour the curve flattens and then dips, because a single robot starts queueing behind its own charging.

This is the sort of answer that is cheap now and expensive later. It costs a few seconds of arithmetic before anybody buys a robot, and a shipped deployment afterwards.

Deliberate differences

What the site changed, and what I changed

A replication is only interesting where it departs from the original on purpose. Each of these is a choice with a reason.

ChangeInstead ofWhy
Colombian colour code A blue, yellow and brown scheme Resolución 2184 de 2019 gives Colombia one national code. Teaching a different one in a village yard is actively harmful.
Proof digest on chain A proof URI alone The original paper names evidence spoofing as an open weakness. Committing the sha256 at submission means the oracle replays the exact bytes the robot committed to, or refuses.
Surplus returns to the poster Keeping the difference in the contract A provider that wins a 100 job at its 60 price is paid 60 and the poster gets 40 back. Otherwise the members who post the most work quietly fund the contract.
A deadline on every task Escrow held indefinitely One crashed robot should not lock a member's money away forever. After the deadline the poster reclaims, and the provider's service is freed.
Matching reads buckets Scanning every task ever posted Open tasks and idle services are indexed by kind with swap and pop removal, and a scan ceiling caps the gas any one transaction can burn. The naive version is fine on day one and broken on day thirty.
The arm is randomised up front Offering a reward to everyone The published study could not attribute its accuracy gain to the incentive, because uptake was too low. Assigning paid and unpaid before the item is seen is what makes the comparison identifiable.
The station has no threads A loop, a worker thread and sleeps It is a state machine that takes a clock and returns effects. A five minute session replays in under a millisecond, so every timeout has a test instead of a stopwatch.
Verification needs no ROS Replaying a rosbag through a live ROS graph The proof is JSON Lines. It can be read by a person during a dispute, hashed in one line, and verified on a laptop that has never had ROS installed.
A geofence check Route length and arm motion only Indoors a corridor is its own boundary. Outdoors it is not, so every logged position has to fall inside the yard the task was posted for.
Both models are one model A separate demo reimplementation The simulation on this page is checked field by field against the Python planner in CI. The random source is 32 bit integer arithmetic precisely so the two agree exactly.

The deployment

What 2,600 metres does to a design

Cold nights, tired batteries

Nights sit near freezing and packs hold less and age faster for it. Drain is a measured parameter in the planner, not a guess: a week of readings at the site goes into the config before any solvency claim is trusted.

Daily rain in the wet season

The station lives under cover, everything outdoors is IP54 or better, and the work area polygon in the oracle's policy is drawn to exclude the part of the yard that floods.

The uplink comes and goes

The chain runs on a laptop in the yard rather than a public network, and the classifier is a chain of backends that falls through rather than one remote call. A dead uplink slows the station down; it does not stop the study.

The nearest spare is a day away

The parts that fail are the light ring, the beam breakers and the SD card, so those travel in duplicate. And the computer is a Raspberry Pi 4, not a 5: the addressable LED driver stack does not support the 5, which is a thing to learn before ordering, not after.

Honest limits

What this is not

The token is not money

ANDE is capped at deployment, closed loop, redeemable only for services inside the yard, and never offered for sale. Nothing in the repository creates an exchange. It is a unit of account for an experiment about whether the accounting works, and the signage on site says exactly that, in Spanish.

The oracle is trusted

An escrow cannot check whether a yard was swept, so one service with one key does. Committing the proof's digest closes the gap where evidence could be swapped afterwards, but it does not make the judgement trustless, and calling the system decentralised would be overselling it. The first thing a second version should attack is requiring several independent verifiers to agree.

The planner is a planner

Open ground, straight line travel, no weather, and nobody changing their mind. Its job is to rule sites out, not to predict what a real Tuesday looks like.

Nothing has run at the site yet

Every number on this page comes from the model or the test suite. The protocol in the repository says what will be measured, what the stopping rules are, and which outcomes would count as negative results, and it was written before the deployment rather than after it.
